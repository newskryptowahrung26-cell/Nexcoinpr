MentalX Founder Mohammad Mousa Highlights UAE Passport’s Historic Rise to No. 1 Globally

Dubai, UAE, November 3rd, 2026

MentalX founder Mohammad Mousa highlights how the UAE passport achieved a historic milestone in December 2018, becoming the world’s most powerful passport after rising from 27th place in just two years. The achievement demonstrated how long-term national vision, strategic planning, secure identity systems, public trust, and disciplined execution can transform a country’s global standing and international recognition.

UAE passport achieved a historic milestone, becoming the world’s most powerful passport and reaching the top position globally after rising from 27th place in just two years. The achievement reflected far more than visa-free access; it demonstrated the effectiveness of a long-term national vision executed with discipline and consistency. 

As CEO of Emirates German Security Printing at the time, I had the privilege of helping lead a key part of that journey. Looking back, what many saw as a passport success story was, in reality, the convergence of seven strategic pillars: National Transformation, Identity & Border Security, Public Trust, Digital Sovereignty, ePassport Modernization, Border-Control Innovation, and Global Recognition.

The most important lesson was simple: trust must be engineered.

A passport is often viewed as a travel document, but for governments it represents something much more significant. It embodies sovereignty, identity, credibility, and trust between nations. Every time a traveler presents a passport at a border crossing, authorities rely on the assumption that the identity behind the document is authentic and that the issuing nation has maintained the highest standards of security and integrity.

For that reason, the UAE’s journey was never about producing a better booklet. It was about strengthening confidence in an entire identity ecosystem.

This became a fundamental component of the UAE’s broader national transformation. As the country pursued its ambition of becoming a global leader in government excellence, innovation, and international connectivity, trusted identity became a strategic enabler. Secure mobility, efficient border operations, and international cooperation all depended upon confidence in the underlying systems that establish and protect identity.

At the same time, what we now call digital sovereignty was becoming increasingly important. Long before the term gained global attention, the UAE recognized that identity systems, travel documents, and border-security capabilities were not merely operational functions. They were strategic national assets. Nations that can secure their identities and control the mechanisms that establish trust are better positioned to protect their interests and strengthen their international standing.

This understanding drove the modernization of the UAE ePassport ecosystem. Many people associate ePassports solely with electronic chips, but modernization extends far beyond technology. It includes document design, advanced security features, authentication mechanisms, production integrity, issuance controls, and compliance with international standards.

The true objective is not to create a more sophisticated document. The objective is to create a document that the world trusts.

The UAE passport’s rise to the top of the global rankings was ultimately the visible result of years of investment in that trust. 

However, even the most secure passport has limited value if its authenticity cannot be verified quickly and accurately at the border.

This realization shaped the next stage of our work: border-control innovation.

As travel volumes increased and document fraud became more sophisticated, border officers required capabilities that could keep pace with emerging threats. Security could no longer rely solely on manual inspection. It needed intelligence-driven verification.

One of the initiatives developed as part of this vision was the rollout of the 7E Reader, a document-authentication platform that was later deployed across UAE airports and Land and Sea Border Crossings. Designed to examine passports, visas, identity cards, and other travel documents using multiple inspection methods, it gave frontline officers the ability to verify authenticity, detect alterations, and identify potential fraud within seconds. Public reports subsequently highlighted its nationwide adoption and operational impact. 

What made this initiative particularly significant was that it represented an early application of principles that today are commonly associated with artificial intelligence.

Years before AI became the dominant topic in government and corporate boardrooms, we were already exploring how advanced analytics, intelligent document examination, automated verification, pattern recognition, and knowledge-based systems could strengthen border security and support decision-making. The objective was never to replace border officers. Rather, it was to augment their expertise by providing faster analysis, greater visibility, and stronger decision support.

That principle remains highly relevant today.

Across industries, many organizations approach AI primarily as a technology initiative. In my experience, the real challenge is not technological. It is organizational and human. Successful adoption depends on trust. Employees must trust the systems, leaders must trust the outcomes, and citizens must trust the institutions deploying them.

Without trust, adoption stalls and without adoption, transformation never reaches scale.

The UAE passport story demonstrates the opposite. Global recognition was achieved because trust was built methodically across every layer of the ecosystem. Security, innovation, governance, execution, and stakeholder alignment advanced together.

The result was not only a world-leading passport. It was international confidence in the systems and institutions behind it. 

Today, as governments and enterprises navigate AI, digital identity, cybersecurity, and increasingly complex transformation agendas, the leadership lesson remains unchanged. Technology will continue to evolve. New capabilities will emerge. Competitive advantages will shift.

Yet sustainable transformation will always depend on the same foundation.

Technology enables transformation. Innovation accelerates transformation. Execution delivers transformation.

But trust is what makes transformation endure.

The rise of the UAE passport to number one globally demonstrated that when national vision, secure identity, border innovation, and public trust move together, extraordinary outcomes become possible. In an AI-driven world, that may be the most important lesson of all.
