Geologist Johnny Tesone Announces Secrets of the Winds, Exploring Earth’s History Through Visuals and Research

LOUISVILLE, CO, November 18th, 2026

Independent researcher, author and inventor draws on 35 years of corporate and independent geoscience experience to document geological formations, fossils and proposed connections between Earth’s history and cosmic phenomena.

Planetary resilience geologist, author, and inventor Johnny Tesone has announced the pre-release of Secrets of the Winds: Discovery Chronicles of Earth’s Galactic Odyssey, a full-scale photographic research journal that explores Earth’s geological history through extensive field documentation, specimen analysis, and investigations into connections between geological processes and cosmic phenomena.

Drawing on 35 years of corporate and independent geoscience experience, Tesone brings together field photography, microscopic material analysis, satellite imagery and geological observations in an extensive visual record of formations, fossils and specimens he believes merit further scientific investigation. Spanning approximately 450 pages and featuring more than 1,000 photographs, the book documents his research and presents interpretations that challenge conventional perspectives on Earth’s geological past.

Tesone earned a Bachelor of Science degree in Geology from the University of Wyoming in 1985 and subsequently spent more than three decades working in corporate geoscience and technology services. His independent research led to the creation of GeoVortex, a platform dedicated to documenting geological observations, presenting research findings and encouraging collaboration among scientists and other qualified investigators.

A Wyoming Expedition That Reshaped His Research

Tesone traces a pivotal shift in his research to a wilderness expedition in Wyoming’s Wind River Range in July 2020. During the expedition, familiar landscapes prompted him to reconsider geological formations he had encountered throughout his life and to investigate their potential significance through a more intensive process of field documentation.

The experience initiated an expanded research effort involving repeat site observations, satellite imagery, specimen photography and detailed examination of geological materials. These investigations became the foundation of Secrets of the Winds, which combines personal accounts, photographic evidence and geological interpretations into a full-scale research journal.

The book invites readers to examine the documented formations and specimens directly, compare observations and engage with the questions raised by Tesone’s research.

Investigating Fossils, Geological Anomalies and the Origins of Life

Among the materials featured in the book is a fossil specimen measuring approximately one centimeter, reportedly collected in Fremont County, Wyoming. GeoVortex describes the specimen as potentially more than 500 million years old and presents Tesone’s investigation into its possible biological significance.

Fremont Co, WY: 1 cm new species fossil, protected discovery material GeoVortex

Tesone has proposed the designation Exterran Infinitas Nimerigarus (EIN) for the material under investigation. His research also explores reported geochemical similarities between ancient fossil material and specimens collected in Colorado.

The book examines these findings alongside broader questions concerning the geological record, the origins and development of life, and the possibility that some formations or specimens may warrant interpretations beyond currently established classifications. The specimen’s age, biological identity and any proposed extraterrestrial connection remain subjects for independent scientific evaluation.

Tesone also investigates major geological questions, including the Great Unconformity, the Cambrian Explosion and a proposed Precambrian upheaval associated with the Yellowstone region. His approach draws on geological observations and concepts from condensed-matter physics and plasma physics to develop and examine alternative interpretations of Earth’s history.

SE Asia; 250-meter-long Plasma Rocket Species

The research journal presents Tesone’s observations, analytical approach and proposed explanations as a basis for further examination, inviting qualified researchers to assess the supporting evidence and determine whether the findings merit additional investigation.

Independent Endorsement and Scientific Collaboration

Retired nuclear physicist and engineer Mike Montgomery has expressed strong support for the work and its potential implications.

“This is real science and the dissertation should be adopted into mainstream science and media. It’s full of information… and reveals the origin of life on the planet.” , Mike Montgomery, (Ret.) Nuclear Physicist & Engineer

Through GeoVortex, Tesone is seeking collaboration with geologists, physicists, materials scientists and other qualified researchers who can independently examine the specimens, evaluate the documentation and contribute expertise to the questions raised by his investigations.

GeoVortex also features approximately 30 research-related videos on its YouTube channel, extending the photographic record into video-based documentation. Tesone is exploring potential partnerships with scientists, documentary producers and production studios to develop a documentary series following his field investigations and the scientific questions they present.

A Lifetime of Exploration Behind the Research

Beyond his professional background in geoscience and technology, Tesone is a lifelong outdoorsman and fly fisherman whose experiences in Wyoming’s wilderness have informed his continuing interest in Earth’s geological record.

Secrets of the Winds: Discovery Chronicles of Earth’s Galactic Odyssey brings together decades of professional experience, independent fieldwork and extensive visual documentation in a project designed to make Tesone’s observations accessible to readers, researchers and potential scientific collaborators.

Through the publication and GeoVortex, Tesone aims to expand discussion around his findings, encourage independent examination of the documented materials and foster collaboration across scientific disciplines.

About Johnny Tesone and GeoVortex

Johnny Tesone is a planetary resilience geologist, author, researcher and inventor with 40 years of corporate and independent geoscience experience. A 1985 graduate of the University of Wyoming with a Bachelor of Science degree in Geology, Tesone founded GeoVortex to document geological observations, share research findings and facilitate collaboration concerning Earth’s ancient history and potential cosmic connections.

His book, Secrets of the Winds: Discovery Chronicles of Earth’s Galactic Odyssey, combines field photography, specimen documentation, geological research and interpretations concerning Earth’s geological past. The book was rerelease after capturing the stunning James Webb Telescope Nebulae photos to Earths fundamentally undiscovered geological record.
