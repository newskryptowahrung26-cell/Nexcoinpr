Big Baller Brand Co-Founder Alan Foster Pledges Half of Any Lawsuit Recovery to Charity

LOS ANGELES, CALIFORNIA, November 25th, 2026

Foster is seeking more than $200 million in a federal trademark case over the MB1 name and says 50 percent of any award will go to charitable causes.

Alan Foster, a co-founder of Big Baller Brand, has pledged to donate half of any money he recovers from his federal trademark lawsuit over the MB1 name to charity. The suit, filed against PUMA North America, NBA player LaMelo Ball and several others, seeks damages of more than $200 million.

At that figure, the pledge would be worth up to $100 million. Foster says the first beneficiaries would be programs that help small business owners defend their intellectual property against larger companies. Youth development, financial education, athletics and support for underserved communities make up the rest of his list.

The MB1 Dispute

Foster, whose legal name is Gregory Alan Foster, filed the case in the U.S. District Court for the Central District of California on November 6, 2023. Along with PUMA and LaMelo Ball, the defendants include Ball’s company MB1 Enterprises, Big Baller Brand Inc., and Ball’s parents, LaVar and Tina Ball.

The case turns on a name. Big Baller Brand, which Foster launched with LaVar Ball in 2016, released LaMelo Ball’s first signature shoe in 2017 and called it the MB1, short for Melo Ball, with the number 1 taken from his jersey. Foster says he came up with that name and design and helped register the trademarks behind it.

LaMelo Ball later signed an endorsement deal with PUMA, and his signature shoe with the company has also been marketed as the MB1. Foster alleges that this use was never authorized. His complaint lists claims for trademark infringement, dilution, unfair business practices, fraud and conversion.

LaMelo Ball signed his endorsement deal with PUMA ahead of the 2020 NBA Draft and has since released several signature models with the company, which has made the line one of the more visible athlete sneaker programs in the league.

His attorney, Raymond Brenneman, told the Los Angeles Times that the $200 million figure was an estimate of merchandise sold by PUMA and Big Baller Brand using trademarks Foster says he owns.

To support the claim, Foster’s filing includes images of PUMA shoes described as the MB1 on several retail websites and in an online article, the newspaper reported.

What the Court Has Said

In August 2024, Judge Fernando L. Aenlle-Rocha turned down a request from LaMelo Ball and MB1 Enterprises to throw out key claims, Bloomberg Law reported. That cleared the way for the case to continue, though the ruling made no finding on whether Foster’s claims are true.

The defendants dispute the lawsuit. LaMelo Ball and MB1 Enterprises have also filed a counterclaim against Foster, according to court records.

Any donation would come only from a judgment or settlement in Foster’s favor, and the final amount could be far lower than what he is seeking. He has not yet named the organizations that would receive the money.

Foster now works as a business coach and strategist in Los Angeles. He wrote about his time at Big Baller Brand in his memoir, Story of Green.

About Alan Foster

Alan Foster is a Los Angeles-based business coach, strategist, consultant and author. He co-founded Big Baller Brand in 2016 and wrote the memoir Story of Green. His biography also credits him as the creator and a consulting producer of the Facebook Watch series Ball in the Family. Learn more at alanfosterofficial.com.
