Easy Lemon Announces New Guide Explaining Vehicle Buybacks Calculated Under Lemon Law

New York, USA, September 25th, 2026

Easy Lemon, the consumer brand of RockPoint Law P.C., has announced the publication of a new guide explaining how vehicle buybacks are calculated under lemon law, including purchase costs, incidental expenses, mileage offsets, outstanding vehicle loans, and alternative settlement options. The guide is designed to help vehicle owners understand the arithmetic behind a manufacturer repurchase before evaluating a proposed settlement.

A vehicle how the buyback figure is calculated is generally more complicated than simply returning a vehicle for the amount originally paid. Depending on the applicable state law and circumstances of the claim, the calculation can involve the vehicle’s purchase price, taxes and fees, certain incidental expenses, a mileage-based usage deduction, and the amount remaining on an auto loan.

Explaining the Buyback Calculation

Easy Lemon’s guide explains that the starting point for a potential repurchase is generally based on the amount actually paid for the vehicle rather than simply the advertised sticker price.

Depending on applicable law, qualifying amounts can include sales tax, registration costs, documentation fees, and certain finance charges. Incidental expenses, such as towing and rental-car costs, may also be relevant in qualifying circumstances.

The guide emphasizes the importance of maintaining documentation for these expenses because missing receipts and records can make it more difficult to establish the amounts associated with a claim.

Understanding the Mileage Offset

The guide also addresses one of the most significant components of many lemon law buyback calculations: the mileage-based usage offset.

State laws differ in how the offset is calculated, but the general concept is that a manufacturer may receive a deduction for the consumer’s use of the vehicle before a qualifying repair attempt or other legally relevant event.

Easy Lemon notes that owners should pay particular attention to the mileage used in the applicable statutory formula. The calculation may be based on mileage at an earlier point in the repair history rather than the mileage on the vehicle when the claim ultimately settles, depending on the state law involved.

How Outstanding Loans Affect a Repurchase

Another key issue addressed in the guide is what happens when a vehicle is financed.

A manufacturer repurchase does not necessarily mean that the consumer receives the entire calculated amount directly. When an outstanding vehicle loan exists, the lender generally has an interest that must be addressed as part of the transaction.

The amount remaining after satisfying the applicable loan obligation can determine how much money, if any, is ultimately paid directly to the vehicle owner.

Easy Lemon’s guide encourages owners to evaluate a proposed buyback based on the overall financial outcome, including whether the transaction resolves the outstanding vehicle debt and accounts for qualifying amounts paid by the consumer.

Cash-and-Keep Settlements

The guide also explains that not every lemon law matter necessarily ends with a vehicle repurchase.

In some circumstances, a consumer may pursue a cash-and-keep settlement, allowing the owner to retain the vehicle while receiving a payment. Whether that option makes financial sense depends on factors including the severity of the defect, the vehicle’s remaining useful life, safety considerations, and the cost of replacing the vehicle.

Easy Lemon notes that consumers evaluating such an option should consider current replacement costs, financing rates, insurance expenses, and the condition of the existing vehicle rather than relying solely on its original purchase price.

Attorney Fees and Lemon Law Claims

Easy Lemon’s guide further discusses attorney-fee provisions associated with consumer warranty and lemon law claims.

The federal Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act and many state lemon law statutes contain provisions that can allow prevailing consumers to recover qualifying attorney fees from a manufacturer. The specific rules and requirements vary by jurisdiction.

Understanding how these provisions operate can be important when consumers evaluate whether pursuing a warranty or lemon law claim is financially practical.

Documentation Can Affect a Claim

Easy Lemon emphasizes that maintaining complete records can be important throughout a lemon law matter.

Vehicle owners are encouraged to retain repair orders, including records from visits where no repair was ultimately performed. Such documents can establish when a problem was reported and the vehicle’s mileage at the time.

Purchase agreements, tax and fee documentation, correspondence with manufacturers or dealers, and other records can also help establish the history and financial aspects of a claim.

State Law Determines the Outcome

Because lemon law requirements differ from state to state, the calculation and eligibility requirements applicable to one vehicle owner may not apply to another.

Issues including repair attempts, days out of service, mileage offsets, warranty periods, statutes of limitations, leased vehicles, and attorney-fee recovery can depend on the jurisdiction and specific facts of a case.

Easy Lemon’s newly announced guide is intended as general educational information and does not replace legal advice regarding an individual claim.
